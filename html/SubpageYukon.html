Yukon Delta

Nature & Biodiversity

The Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta is one of the largest river deltas in the world, located where the Yukon and Kuskokwim rivers empty into the Bering Sea in western Alaska. It is a vast, treeless landscape dominated by tundra, wetlands, and thousands of small lakes.

This region is a critical breeding ground for millions of migratory birds, including the Emperor Goose and various species of swans and sandpipers. The surrounding waters are rich with salmon, seals, and walruses, making it one of the most productive subarctic ecosystems on Earth.

History & Culture

For thousands of years, the delta has been the homeland of the Yup'ik and Cup'ik people. Their culture is deeply tied to the rhythms of the land and sea, with a lifestyle centered around subsistence fishing, hunting, and gathering.

Today, the region remains sparsely populated, with small villages accessible only by plane or boat (and snowmobile in the winter). Despite the challenges of climate change and permafrost melt, the indigenous communities continue to preserve their linguistic heritage and traditional knowledge, maintaining a profound spiritual connection to the delta's shifting waters.
