Roughly the size of Louisiana, making it one of the world's largest deltas.

100+ Species

Millions of birds migrate here annually from every continent on Earth.

Subarctic

A landscape of permafrost, tundra, and "drunken forests."

Nature & Biodiversity

The Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta is a vast, treeless landscape dominated by tundra and thousands of small lakes. This region is a critical breeding ground for the Emperor Goose and various species of swans.

The surrounding waters are rich with all five species of Pacific Salmon, which serve as the lifeblood for both the local wildlife and the human inhabitants.

History & Culture

For thousands of years, the delta has been the homeland of the Yup'ik and Cup'ik people. Their culture is deeply tied to subsistence fishing and hunting.

Environmental Note: The delta is currently on the front lines of climate change. Rising sea levels and melting permafrost are causing significant coastal erosion, threatening many ancestral villages.

Regional Economy

Commercial fishing is the primary industry in the delta. Because there are no roads connecting these villages to the outside world, the economy relies heavily on air freight and summer barge shipments.
